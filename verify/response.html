If something goes wrong — citation view

What to do if you think you've been hit with ransomware, phishing, or an account takeover. [guidance] Read it before you need it. [guidance]

Plain view →

If something's happening right now, call your campus IT help desk from a phone or another device you trust, and do what they say. [guidance] In the U.S. you can also report to CISA and the FBI IC3. [2] This page is general educational material, not professional incident-response advice. [guidance]

In the moment

  • If a message, login page, or pop-up looks suspicious, stop. [guidance] Don't click, don't enter credentials. [guidance]
  • If you already clicked or entered a password, change that password from a different device and report it now. [guidance]
  • If your device is acting strange (files renamed, ransom note, unexpected encryption), disconnect it from Wi-Fi and any cables, leave it powered on, and contact IT. [authority: CISA]
  • Forward suspicious emails using the campus 'Report Phishing' button or as an attachment to the security team. [guidance]
  • Don't try to 'fix' a suspected infection yourself. [guidance] Don't pay a ransom on a personal device. [guidance] Call IT. [guidance]
  • Follow official guidance from the campus emergency channel. [guidance] Ignore unverified rumors on social media. [guidance]

After the dust settles

  • Change passwords for any account that may have been exposed and review login history. [guidance]
  • Re-enable backups and confirm your important files are still recoverable. [guidance]
  • Tell classmates and roommates what you saw. [1] Phishing campaigns usually target many students at once. [guidance]
  • Attend any post-incident briefings. [guidance] They teach you the exact lure that worked so you can spot the next one. [guidance]
