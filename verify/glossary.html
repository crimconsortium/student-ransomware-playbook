Glossary — citation view

Plain-language definitions for the terms in this playbook. [guidance]

Plain view →

After-action review (AAR)
A structured, blameless review held after an incident to capture what happened, what worked, what didn't, and what to change. [guidance] Produces a written report with tracked actions. [guidance]
Backups (immutable / offline)
Copies of data that can't be altered or deleted by a normal admin (immutable) or are physically disconnected (offline). [guidance] Critical for ransomware recovery. [guidance]
CISA
U.S. [guidance] Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. [1] Publishes the #StopRansomware guide and maintains free resources for higher education. [guidance]
Command and control (C2)
Infrastructure attackers use to communicate with malware on compromised systems. [guidance] Blocking C2 helps stop ongoing attacks. [guidance]
Containment
Steps that stop an attack from spreading further: isolating hosts, disabling accounts, revoking sessions, blocking outbound traffic. [guidance]
Decryption key
The key needed to reverse ransomware encryption. [guidance] May or may not be supplied by attackers after payment; sometimes published by law enforcement. [guidance]
Detection
Identifying that an intrusion or attack is occurring or has occurred, ideally before damage is done. [guidance]
Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)
Software that monitors laptops, desktops, and servers for malicious behavior and supports investigation and response. [guidance]
FERPA
Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (U.S.). [2] Governs the privacy of student education records and shapes notification obligations. [guidance]
FIDO2 / WebAuthn
Phishing-resistant authentication standards. [3] Hardware keys and platform authenticators (Touch ID, Face ID, Windows Hello) implement them. [guidance]
Incident Commander
The single person responsible for directing the response during an incident, regardless of normal seniority. [guidance]
Indicator of Compromise (IOC)
An observable artifact (file hash, IP, domain, behavior) that suggests a system has been attacked. [guidance]
MFA / Multi-factor authentication
Requiring more than a password to sign in, typically a code or hardware key. [guidance] Phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn) binds the credential to the real site, so it cannot be replayed on a fake login page. [3]
MFA fatigue
Attack where an attacker repeatedly triggers MFA prompts hoping the user approves one out of frustration. [4]
NIST
U.S. [5] National Institute of Standards and Technology. [guidance] Publishes the Cybersecurity Framework and SP 800-61 for incident response. [guidance]
Phishing
Deceptive messages (email, SMS, voice, web) that trick users into entering credentials, running malware, or approving fraudulent transactions. [guidance]
Ransomware
Malware that encrypts files and/or steals data, then demands payment to restore access or to prevent publication. [guidance]
RDP
Remote Desktop Protocol. [guidance] Frequently abused by attackers when exposed to the internet without strong controls. [guidance]
Segmentation
Dividing a network into zones so a compromise in one area can't spread freely to others. [guidance]
Tabletop exercise
A facilitated discussion-based drill where leaders walk through their response to a hypothetical incident. [guidance]
Third-party risk
Risk introduced by vendors, SaaS providers, and contractors. [guidance] Recent higher-ed ransomware impact has been driven heavily by exploited third-party software. [6]
