Student Ransomware Playbook

A plain-language guide for college and university students. How to spot phishing, protect your accounts and coursework, and what to do when something goes wrong. A few simple habits make you a much harder target, and help protect everyone around you. Written for students, not IT departments.

Protect yourself

A handful of habits (MFA, backups, updates, spotting phishing) that make your accounts much harder to crack.

If something goes wrong

What to do if you clicked a phishing link, saw a ransom note, or think your account's been compromised. Read it before you need it.

Checklist

A short self-audit, about five minutes. Progress saves on this device only.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used in this playbook.

FAQ

Straight answers to questions students actually ask, including what counts as a real incident.
About

About this resource

The Student Ransomware Playbook is by Joshua Gerstenfeld and Scott Jacques with support from the CrimRxiv Consortium. It gets a quarterly content review against CISA, EDUCAUSE, and NIST guidance.

Code is MIT-licensed. Text, diagrams, and illustrations are CC BY 4.0. The repo is at github.com/crimconsortium/student-ransomware-playbook.

A note on AI. This site was initially drafted with the help of AI — primarily Claude (Anthropic), used through Perplexity Computer — for both the text and the code. The authors then reviewed and edited every page, checked the guidance against the cited public sources, and take full responsibility for the claims and recommendations on this site.

Educational resource, not for use during a live incident

This site is a plain-language summary of public guidance for advance reading. If you think an incident is happening right now, call your campus IT help desk from a phone or another device you trust. In the U.S., you can also report to CISA #StopRansomware and the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center. Nothing here is professional incident-response advice, legal advice, or a substitute for trained responders or your school's own policies.
